Research

E-commerce Expansion and Local Unemployment: Evidence from COVID-19

Working Paper

This paper examines whether the rapid expansion of e-commerce during the COVID-19 pandemic increased local unemployment in the United States. Using county-level data, I measure local exposure through payroll growth in seven retail industries with substantial e-commerce activity. To address the endogeneity of local growth, I employ a shift-share instrumental variable that combines pre-existing local industry shares with national industry-level growth. The analysis also considers commuting zones to assess whether the findings extend to broader local labor markets.

The results indicate that expansion in these industries increased local unemployment during the initial pandemic period, while estimates for subsequent periods suggest that this effect was temporary. Industry-level patterns and evidence on worker transitions are consistent with labor reallocation: growth in online-related activities coincided with job losses and displacement in traditional retail. These findings highlight the short-run adjustment costs of the shift toward online consumption and show why growth in expanding sectors may not immediately offset employment losses elsewhere.

Vertically Integrated Contract Manufacturing under Technology Leakage

Published in 산업조직연구, 2025

This paper studies how technology-leakage risk shapes competition and organizational choices in contract manufacturing. A vertically integrated manufacturer may use a client's technology to enter the client's product market, creating a potential conflict of interest. Using a two-period model of competition between vertically integrated and independent contract manufacturers, the analysis examines how clients' exposure to leakage affects pricing and equilibrium profits. The results show that differences in leakage risk can differentiate manufacturers and soften price competition. Under the conditions identified in the model, vertical integration can therefore remain profitable despite clients' concerns about technology leakage. The paper highlights how technological risk interacts with market competition to shape firms' incentives to integrate or separate their activities.

자영업 구조 변화가 지역 노동시장에 미치는 영향: 코로나19 기간을 중심으로

Published in 산업경제연구, 2026

This paper investigates how changes in self-employment during the COVID-19 pandemic affected local labor markets in South Korea. The analysis combines a national time-series approach with a regional empirical design. It revisits earlier vector autoregression evidence using an extended sample covering 2015–2023 and employs a Bartik instrumental variable approach with city-, county-, and district-level data. The regional results indicate that areas with higher self-employment shares in 2020 subsequently experienced lower growth in individually insured National Pension subscribers. This outcome captures an aspect of local labor-market adjustment through pension participation, rather than measuring unemployment directly. The findings highlight the vulnerability of regions more dependent on self-employment and connect changes in local economic structure to social insurance coverage during the pandemic.

Econometric Methods

My research applies instrumental variables and two-stage least squares, including shift-share (Bartik) instruments, to study the effects of industry-level changes on local economic outcomes. I have worked with fixed-effects specifications, clustered standard errors, and robustness checks using alternative exposure measures and geographic units. My identification assessments include pre-period falsification tests, comparisons across event windows, Rotemberg-weight diagnostics, and LIML estimation as a robustness check for instrumental-variable results.

I also use Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) models for firm-level borrowing-demand estimation and forecasting. This work involves constructing lagged financial indicators, evaluating alternative outcome definitions, treating missing values and outliers, and assessing the sensitivity of aggregate forecasts to modeling assumptions. Additional experience includes vector autoregression (VAR) and linked worker-level analysis of employment transitions.

Large-Scale Data Experience

I have processed corporate financial text files of approximately 56 GB and 93 GB, using file splitting and partitioned RDS storage to manage large datasets. In one financial-data workflow, I reshaped approximately 43.6 million account-level records into 7.57 million firm–accounting-date observations covering approximately 1.53 million firms. The work included checking identifier consistency, assessing missing values, and constructing financial indicators for analysis. A subsequent borrowing-demand analysis used approximately 3 million observations.

My academic research also combines U.S. county- and industry-level data with local economic indicators and linked household survey records. This includes constructing geographic crosswalks, aggregating county data to commuting zones, and examining worker transitions across industries and occupations.

Project: Estimating Technology Credit Guarantee Demand and Assessing Funding Requirements
Commissioned by the Korea Technology Finance Corporation (KIBO)

I support a commissioned research project on technology credit guarantee demand and the financial resources needed to sustain guarantee provision. The project examines observed, potential, and policy-driven demand, alongside appropriate and sustainable ratios of outstanding guarantees to the institution's capital base.

My work focuses on building and analyzing large-scale firm-level financial datasets in R. Responsibilities include processing raw data, constructing firm-level panels, checking data consistency, and developing financial indicators for borrowing-demand estimation. I apply PPML models to estimate firm-level borrowing demand and produce aggregate forecasts, including projections for 2027.

I also compare alternative definitions of borrowing demand, including total borrowing balances and net increases in borrowing, and assess the sensitivity of forecasts to modeling assumptions. I prepare analytical summaries, tables, figures, and methodological documentation to support the project report and discussions of guarantee demand and funding requirements.
